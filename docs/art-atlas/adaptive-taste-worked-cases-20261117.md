# Worked cases for adaptive taste

All answers here are hypothetical. Artwork identities, source reviews, and the specified live question types were checked against the repository. Some comparisons are proposed next measurements. The conditions for offering them are part of the example.

## 1. One Warhol stands out

**Observation.** Suppose the participant places *Gold Marilyn Monroe* at 98 and the closest other painting at 71. They report high admiration of making and high personal appeal separately. MoMA identifies the image as a screenprinted face from a *Niagara* publicity still, surrounded by a broad gold field. This work has one image of Marilyn. Its first-150 place is proposed. [MoMA, Gold Marilyn Monroe](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/79737)

**Rivals and next question.** A close, recognizable face may carry the response. So might the large field, the photograph-derived graphic treatment, knowledge of Marilyn, or the choice to print her image this way. Compare overall appeal on two eligible cross-artist works first. Vermeer's *Girl with a Pearl Earring* supplies close framing with modelled light and material detail. Rothko's *Orange and Yellow* supplies broad colour fields with no face. Those two candidates differ in several further respects, so a pair of them by itself cannot earn the label “face versus field”. A looser exploratory group could include Malevich's *Painterly Realism of a Football Player* to sample flat shapes without a face. Offer a diagnostic pair only after the production reproduction of the Warhol and its partners have known, opposed features and enough shared controls. [Mauritshuis, Girl with a Pearl Earring](https://www.mauritshuis.nl/en/our-collection/artworks/670-girl-with-a-pearl-earring) · [Buffalo AKG, Orange and Yellow](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/naranja-y-amarillo-mark-rothko/eQEGSGtYwuS37A)

**Answers and reading.** An unfamiliar close portrait high and two colour-field examples low make the framing hypothesis worth keeping. The reverse keeps the fields in play. If every comparison is low, keep the strong response local and ask which of cultural recognition, the specific face or photographic treatment would change the next choice. If the person reports high making admiration alongside low appeal on another Pop work, record the separation directly. Testing serial treatment requires a work that actually repeats the face, such as *Marilyn Diptych*. Its different layout and palette also matter. [Tate, Marilyn Diptych](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/warhol-marilyn-diptych-t03093)

> *Gold Marilyn Monroe* stood out for you, and you also admired its making. The close portrait by Vermeer appealed when the gold field was absent. That makes the framing worth checking on a portrait you have never seen. Your response to Marilyn herself and to the printed treatment is still open.

**Overclaim to reject.** “You love Pop Art and the hypnotic repetition of Warhol's Marilyn.”

## 2. The same artist can produce a favourite and a dislike

**Observation.** Suppose the participant gives Vermeer's *Girl with a Pearl Earring* 94 and *Girl with the Red Hat* 17 under the same anchored question, then explicitly selects the first as a favourite and the second as disliked. Preserve the 77-point difference and both later declarations. These works share close framing, controlled rendering and material detail in the current reviews. The palette, lighting, costume, support, narrative detail and rhythm differ. [Mauritshuis, Girl with a Pearl Earring](https://www.mauritshuis.nl/en/our-collection/artworks/670-girl-with-a-pearl-earring) · [National Gallery of Art, Girl with the Red Hat](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/60-girl-red-hat)

**Rivals and next question.** One possibility is a response to light and the quiet silhouette. Another is a response specific to the face, dress, familiarity, or size of this reproduction. The within-Vermeer contrast already weakens “Vermeer appeals across treatments”. Try a bounded cross-artist framing test, for example Frans Hals's *Portrait of a Woman, probably Aeltje Dircksdr. Pater*, which retains the near-view portrait and changes the light and edges. Pair it with an adequately matched work by a different artist. If no second candidate can separate the light from other treatment, use the wider group and keep the causes open. Lamps and shadow do not turn the original Vermeer pair into a lighting experiment. [Cleveland Museum of Art, Frans Hals](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1948.137)

**Answers and reading.** If a cross-artist close portrait earns high appeal without the same lighting, close framing remains plausible as one contributor. If other close portraits stay low and a different subject with similarly selective light is high, the portrait explanation weakens. If both are low, “close portraits” drops out as a recurring explanation from this evidence. Even a persuasive outcome cannot identify the role of recognition unless the participant reports it.

> Two Vermeer portraits felt very different to you. *Girl with a Pearl Earring* was an explicit favourite. You disliked *Girl with the Red Hat*. The closeness of the face can't explain that gap on its own. Your response to the next portrait will help place the boundary.

**Overclaim to reject.** “You like Vermeer except when he uses dramatic light.”

## 3. Bosch alongside a spare image

**Observation.** Suppose *The Garden of Earthly Delights* rates 97, Josef Albers's *Light Grey Wall* 90 and another visually dense scene 42. The first has fantastical details and activity across several zones. The second has a few rectangles and precise divisions. Both can reward sustained inspection for different reasons. [Prado, The Garden of Earthly Delights](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/the-garden-of-earthly-delights-triptych/02388242-6d6a-4e9e-a992-e1311eab3609) · [INBAL, Light Grey Wall](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/light-grey-wall-josef-albers/kwFNJMbUtg6DjA)

**Rivals and next question.** Possible routes include fantastical narrative areas in Bosch and clear pattern in Albers. Structured rhythm might be a shared feature. If both were shown together with these levels, the gap between them is small and composition alone explains little about their success. Compare another densely organised work without overt fantasy and a spare work without regular geometry. Mondrian's *Composition with Red, Yellow, and Blue* and *The Night Café* offer further treatment variation once their shared features are reviewed. If the current pool lacks a crossed pair and the new set would vary density, subject, colour and gesture together, select broader exploration. Reserve later diagnostic space for the actual visual alternatives.

**Answers and reading.** High appeal for another dense fantastical work and a geometric work supports two local routes. High appeal at both densities in unrelated comparisons weakens a general “spare composition” description. A dense work with figures and story clues that lands low is a real boundary, especially when fresh works cross the relevant qualities. Which property within each route does the work remains open.

> You gave high appeal to the crowded world of *The Garden of Earthly Delights* and the spare geometry of *Light Grey Wall*. The two paintings put limits on a single preference for density. Check whether imagined scenes and regular geometry recur in different works.

**Overclaim to reject.** “You prefer simple compositions.”

## 4. An unsettling work worth revisiting

**Observation.** Suppose the return map records *Saturn Devouring His Son* at 94 for felt unease and 88 for wish to return. *The Third of May* sits at 86 and 21, respectively. The earlier Saturn appeal line could independently read 82. The works share an artist and differ in what the viewer reports wanting to do. [Prado, Saturn](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/saturn/18110a75-b0e7-430c-bc73-2a4d55893bd6) · [Prado, The Third of May](https://www.museodelprado.es/coleccion/obra-de-arte/el-3-de-mayo-en-madrid-o-los-fusilamientos/5e177409-2993-4240-97fb-847a02c6496c)

**Rivals and next question.** Unease could accompany curiosity about an image. The concentrated encounter in *Saturn* could also earn the return with no broader affinity for unease. Use the same map on *Guernica* and *Collective Suicide* in another set. A different artist and work can separate an item-specific response from a recurring combination. It cannot tell why the person wants to return. [Reina Sofía, Guernica](https://www.museoreinasofia.es/en/collections/artwork/guernica-0/) · [MoMA, Collective Suicide](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/79146)

**Answers and reading.** If *Guernica* gets 85/82 and *Collective Suicide* 84/27, the split recurs across two separately answered groups. If both unsettling works draw low intention to return, keep the positive route local to *Saturn*. A choice to revisit says nothing about how long a person looked.

> You would revisit *Saturn* and *Guernica*, both of which you found unsettling. You felt similar unease about *The Third of May* and *Collective Suicide* and would leave those after one look. Unease can accompany the wish to return. What determined the different choices is still open.

**Overclaim to reject.** “Violent paintings keep you looking.”

## 5. Connection before understanding

**Observation.** Suppose the connection map puts Doré's *Don Quixote in his library* at 24 for understanding and 86 for connection. Brueghel and Rubens's *The Sense of Hearing* sits at 27 and 20.

**Rivals and next question.** A partly understood scene can still feel personal. The room, the reader or the association with books may account for most of this response. Return to the same direct map. Include the known example and use different artists for the new works. If their earlier appeal is comparable, Varo's *Creation of the Birds*, Carrington's *The Distractions of Dagobert* and Vermeer's *The Milkmaid* are real alternatives for a first test.

**Answers and reading.** A similarly hard-to-understand work by Varo with strong connection strengthens the distinction across works. High connection only with a clear *Milkmaid*, paired with distance from the other obscure works, keeps the first result local. The map records how clear the image feels and how personal the viewer finds it. A motive for any one reaction still needs the person's account or a narrower check.

> *Don Quixote in his library* felt personal even though you found it hard to understand. *The Sense of Hearing* felt similarly difficult and more distant. Compare the two judgments again on another set to see whether that separation travels beyond this room and reader.

**Overclaim to reject.** “You connect deeply with ambiguity.”

## 6. Appeal and the purpose of a room

**Observation.** Suppose *The Ambassadors* rates 93 and Hammershøi's *A Room in the Artist's Home* 62. Both were presented in the museum exercise. The person hangs Hammershøi beside *Las Meninas* and *The Arnolfini Portrait* in a room called “Rooms”, and leaves Holbein off the walls. Keep the rest of the actual room and wall state, the question's purpose, the original ratings and the presented IDs.

**Rivals and next question.** Hammershøi could suit the group because of its layered interior or quiet handling. Holbein could have fit but duplicated another work, belonged elsewhere, or had no space. Ask which would go beside the two captured room anchors. The participant can choose either, both, neither or unsure. Treat the offered positions as equivalent for that check. The invitation earns its place because it can change the room-specific reading. [National Gallery, The Ambassadors](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/hans-holbein-the-younger-the-ambassadors)

**Answers and reading.** Hammershøi alone gives the fit explanation support. Holbein alone weakens it. Both leaves redundancy, wall space and room purpose open. Neither or uncertainty keeps the motive open. The next relationship test can keep the same room anchors and compare Cassatt's *In the Omnibus* with Vermeer's *The Milkmaid*. The current reviews cross spatial layering with material detail. The two also differ in activity, palette and period. A one-work choice would move one explanation up without settling the original motive. [Cleveland Museum of Art, In the Omnibus](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1941.71) · [Rijksmuseum, The Milkmaid](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/SK-A-2344)

> Beside *Las Meninas* and *The Arnolfini Portrait*, you chose Hammershøi again, although you had rated *The Ambassadors* higher for personal appeal. That choice tells us how these works fit this room. Spatial layers and the room's quieter treatment both remain plausible.

**Overclaim to reject.** “Leaving Holbein out shows that you value harmony over liking.”

## 7. A note that adds appreciation at the top of the scale

**Observation.** Suppose *Hope* gets 100 on personal appeal. The participant agrees that stories rarely change their appreciation. Tate's account of the one remaining string appears in the exact sourced note. The person answers “Much more” to “How much does knowing this change your appreciation?” Both the prior answer of 100 and this separate report are valid. [Tate, Hope](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/hope-george-frederic-watts/CgGv3RqPFUZk4A?hl=en)

**Rivals and next question.** This detail might have changed what the person saw in this work. Extra time and another look might also matter. The exact same direct-change choices after the sourced skull note on *The Ambassadors* give another work-specific report.

**Answers and reading.** “No change” for Holbein leaves *Hope* a specific exception. “Much more” for both supports two reports of added appreciation under those notes. “Unsure” remains undecided. A randomized comparison with extra-looking controls would be needed to distinguish kinds of context and the effect of viewing again.

> You said stories rarely change what you appreciate. *Hope* was an exception. You had put it at the top for personal appeal and then reported much more appreciation after reading about the remaining string. The note about *The Ambassadors* left your appreciation unchanged.

**Overclaim to reject.** “Reading the story raised your appeal rating past 100 and showed that you misjudged your own taste.”

## 8. Attraction with an unsure artist match

**Observation.** Suppose *Sunflowers* has an anchored appeal of 92 and qualifies for the artist question. The participant leaves its artist unsure. A different participant may match it correctly from the offered labels. Under the current task, the source of the correct match is unmeasured. In a saved task with a guess response, keep “guessed”. Label feedback later in the quiz is a separate, recorded exposure. [National Gallery, Sunflowers](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/vincent-van-gogh-sunflowers)

**Rivals and next question.** Colour and the repeated flower shapes could carry the appeal. Familiarity or Van Gogh's visible handling could contribute. If knowing prior familiarity would change the case, ask “Before Art Atlas, had you seen this exact painting?” with Yes, No and Not sure. Compare Van Gogh's *The Night Café* with Renoir's *Roses in a Vase* after measuring familiarity or retaining it as unknown. The Renoir is a later ordinary reservation. Without an eligible picture that meaningfully crosses the proposed distinctions, select a broader question and keep the live issue for the next block. [Cleveland Museum of Art, Roses in a Vase](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1941.14)

**Answers and reading.** If the person explicitly reports that the Renoir is new and its appeal is also high, a flower still life can work without recognizing the artist or image. If only *The Night Café* appeals, investigate shared handling on other subjects, with the role of knowledge still open. A cued match has item-specific use even when it adds no taste evidence.

> *Sunflowers* was a strong choice, and you left the artist match unsure. A flower still life you have never seen would help show whether the appeal travels outside this painting.

**Overclaim to reject.** “An accurate match shows that you are a Post-Impressionist expert.”

## 9. Landscape subject versus open space

**Observation.** A real comparison from the local selector uses a synthetic 98 for Monet's *Water Lilies (Agapanthus)* and 10 for *The Ambassadors*. Compressing the same ordering to 98 and 97 leaves the possible distinction open. Monet carries both the recorded natural-setting and open-expanse qualities.

**Rivals and next question.** Segantini's *Pine Tree* has a natural setting with open expanse reviewed absent. Van Gogh's *Sunflowers* has open expanse with a natural setting reviewed absent. The works differ on layered space among twelve other jointly reviewed qualities. Their subjects, handling and likely familiarity also differ. This is a feasible pair under the screen once both reproductions are eligible. [Cleveland Museum of Art, Pine Tree](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1982.124)

**Answers and reading.** High *Pine Tree* and low *Sunflowers* gives natural settings more support here. A reversed gap gives open expanse more support. Two low scores leave the Monet response local even with a large difference between the pair. Equal placements leave the alternatives open. Later, an independently selected high landscape from a different period helps distinguish the subject from an artist or technique.

> *Pine Tree* appealed more in the follow-up to *Water Lilies*. A natural setting has more support here than open space. A seascape with equally concentrated marks would help check how far that goes.

**Overclaim to reject.** “You like landscapes because they are spacious.”
