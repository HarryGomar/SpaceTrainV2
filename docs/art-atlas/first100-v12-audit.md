# First 100 comparison-first allocation audit

One deterministic scripted allocation; no participant evidence. Later groups depend on actual earlier choices.

The current source catalogue has 487 records. Default routing has 347 main collection records and 140 world collection records; 344 main records are study-eligible. The first 72 contain 71 Western/Americas works and 16 Mexican works. Great Wave is the sole early global exception.

The fixed ordinary-comparison cohort through 100 contains 127 distinct works: 126 from Western/Americas contexts and 30 with country recorded as Mexico, plus one recorded as Mexico/United States. No deferred East Asian, South Asian or Himalayan work is in this main cohort. All named Notion anchors and all 24 new canonical additions occur in it. Doré is a representative Quixote library design, not a claim that a particular impression was requested. Hope is the identified Tate 1886 version.

## Actual first 50 quartets

### journey-001 · Four different ways of holding a scene

- The Starry Night · Vincent van Gogh
- Mona Lisa · Leonardo da Vinci
- The Great Wave off Kanagawa · Katsushika Hokusai
- The Kiss · Gustav Klimt

- Compare a focal figure, a pair, a sea scene and a night setting.
- Keep recognisability separate from colour and repeated shapes.

### journey-003 · People, light and everyday objects

- The Night Watch · Rembrandt van Rijn
- Girl with a Pearl Earring · Johannes Vermeer
- The Milkmaid · Johannes Vermeer
- Portrait of Frida Kahlo (Retrato de Frida Kahlo) · Diego Rivera

- Compare dramatic lighting with quieter figure treatments.
- Separate a person being present from relationships or objects supplying the interest.

### journey-005 · Abstraction can be sparse or dense

- Mansión del agua [Water Mansion] · Günther Gerzso
- Composition 8 · Wassily Kandinsky
- Convergence · Jackson Pollock
- Composition with Red, Yellow, and Blue · Piet Mondrian

- Keep abstraction separate from visible density.
- Compare geometric order with accumulating marks and rhythm.

### journey-007 · Objects and surrounding space

- Twilight in the Wilderness · Frederic Edwin Church
- Still Life with Foot · Rufino Tamayo
- Still Life · María Blanchard
- Sunflowers · Vincent van Gogh

- Contrast object-centred compositions with an extensive depicted setting.
- Compare the treatment of tangible forms rather than equating stillness with emptiness.

### journey-010 · Close relationships and public company

- The Child’s Bath · Mary Cassatt
- Madre niña · David Alfaro Siqueiros
- The Cradle · Berthe Morisot
- In the Omnibus · Mary Cassatt

- Compare intimate care with figures sharing a public setting.
- Two Cassatt works offer a limited within-artist contrast; subject and handling still differ.

### journey-012 · Dramatic lighting is not bodily action

- The Taking of Christ · Caravaggio
- Stag at Sharkey's · George Bellows
- Saudade (Longing) · Almeida Júnior
- Judith Beheading Holofernes · Caravaggio

- Compare strenuous action with a quiet figure under selective light.
- Two Caravaggios give a limited same-artist comparison without isolating a single visual cause.

### journey-013 · Groups can be staged or incidental

- The Calling of Saint Matthew · Caravaggio
- The School of Athens · Raphael
- Estado mayor de bufones · José Clemente Orozco
- The Last Supper · Leonardo da Vinci

- Compare arranged group encounters with different kinds of bodily gesture.
- Keep a staged scene separate from precision of rendering.

### journey-014 · A spare image can suggest a story

- Harmony (Suggestive Self-Portrait) · Remedios Varo
- Hope · George Frederic Watts and assistants
- The Sleeping Gypsy · Henri Rousseau
- The Scream · Edvard Munch

- Compare sparse suggestive imagery with a room full of narrative clues.
- Keep impossible imagery, symbolic context and distortion distinct.

### journey-016 · A room can organise a story or stay quiet

- The Arnolfini Portrait · Jan van Eyck
- The Ambassadors · Hans Holbein the Younger
- Las Meninas · Diego Velázquez
- A Room in the Artist’s Home in Strandgade, Copenhagen, with the Artist’s Wife · Vilhelm Hammershøi

- Compare elaborate interior encounters with a restrained inhabited room.
- Keep surrounding space separate from the number of objects or secondary clues.

### journey-020 · Restrained compositions use different structures

- Orange and Yellow · Mark Rothko
- Cobalto y Blanco · Carmen Herrera
- Black Square · Kazimir Malevich
- Prometheus Bound · Barnett Newman

- Compare sparse abstractions without treating all abstraction as one style.
- Distinguish repeated structure from sharp and soft boundaries.

### journey-024 · Landscapes and a compressed setting

- Wanderer above the Sea of Fog · Caspar David Friedrich
- Valle de México desde el cerro de Santa Isabel · José María Velasco
- Mount Sainte-Victoire · Paul Cezanne
- The World's Wound (Der Gespaltene) · Peter Birkhäuser

- Compare extensive scenery with a close, altered arrangement.
- Keep a dramatic setting separate from a crowded narrative.

### journey-026 · Story clues can accumulate or concentrate

- Don Quixote in his library · Gustave Doré, engraved by Héliodore Pisan
- The Sense of Hearing · Jan Brueghel the Elder and Peter Paul Rubens
- El hombre controlador del universo · Diego Rivera
- David with the Head of Goliath · Caravaggio

- Compare clues concentrated around a figure with distributed objects and incidents.
- A dense scene and a symbolic subject need not be the same preference.

### journey-028 · Dense does not mean fantastical

- The Garden of Earthly Delights · Hieronymus Bosch
- Composition VII · Wassily Kandinsky
- The Triumph of Death · Pieter Bruegel the Elder
- Hunters in the Snow · Pieter Bruegel the Elder

- Compare detailed observed scenes, imagined incidents and dense abstraction.
- Keep density separate from whether figures and events are legible.

### journey-030 · Different amounts of space for an image to imply meaning

- The Temptations of Saint Anthony the Abbot · Joachim Patinir and Quinten Massys
- The Sense of Sight · Jan Brueghel the Elder and Peter Paul Rubens
- Calavera Garbancera, later known as La Catrina · José Guadalupe Posada
- Saturn Devouring His Son · Francisco de Goya

- Compare a compact graphic figure with extensive scenes and gathered objects.
- Documented symbolism remains context, not a visual score inferred from the title.

### journey-033 · Physical action and energetic handling

- Liberty Leading the People · Eugène Delacroix
- Collective Suicide · David Alfaro Siqueiros
- The Burning of the Houses of Lords and Commons, 16 October 1834 · Joseph Mallord William Turner
- Return from the Horse Fair · Rosa Bonheur

- Compare visible bodily action with activity carried by marks, terrain and colour.
- Unresolved small figures stay unknown in the literal action descriptor.

### journey-037 · Figures, settings and unusual bodies

- The Night Café · Vincent van Gogh
- Ophelia · John Everett Millais
- Echo of a Scream · David Alfaro Siqueiros
- Las dos Fridas (The Two Fridas) · Frida Kahlo

- Compare surrounding place with confrontational or doubled figures.
- Separate visible bodily impossibility from emotional tone and a narrated tragedy.

### journey-039 · Social scenes through different handling

- A Sunday on La Grande Jatte — 1884 · Georges Seurat
- Sorrowful Friday · María Izquierdo
- The Banjo Lesson · Henry Ossawa Tanner
- Dance · Henri Matisse

- Compare everyday group relationships across precise and altered treatment.
- The paired admiration question asks about making separately from preference.

### journey-041 · Precise edges across imagined and abstract forms

- Zapatista Landscape (The Guerrilla) · Diego Rivera
- Creación de las aves (Creation of the Birds) · Remedios Varo
- The Distractions of Dagobert · Leonora Carrington
- Carnaval d’Arlequin (Carnival of Harlequin) · Joan Miró

- Compare meticulous imagined scenes with organic and geometric abstraction.
- Clear edges remain common here, so precision is not treated as the opposite of abstraction.
