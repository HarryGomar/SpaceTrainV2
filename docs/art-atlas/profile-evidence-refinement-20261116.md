# Whole-profile evidence and focused follow-ups

The v16 change separates evidence about a particular response from permission to describe overall taste. It preserves completed instruments and answers; historical ordering coordinates do not become absolute liking, and direct context-change answers do not become fabricated after-ratings.

## Analysis changes

- Admiration of making has its own reading and cannot supply the overall taste heading. A local comparative pattern can remain useful without becoming a whole-profile explanation.
- Candidate explanations carry their fit with the complete available preference record: declared favourites with and without the quality, explicit dislikes sharing it, rated works with and without it, and the broader anchored appeal association. Unknown reviews stay unknown. The different sources remain linked rather than being counted as independent confirmations.
- A shared quality of favourites is described separately from a broader appeal association. A quality can connect exceptional favourites without explaining general ratings. Both supporting examples and exceptions remain available.
- Response maps can reveal a difference when the two paintings have similar personal appeal but different reported attention, or similar admiration but different appeal. Neither painting needs to be disliked. One best contrast per artwork set is retained; single-set differences stay provisional. These are reports, not measured viewing time or causal explanations.
- Museum omissions propose a room-fit explanation only as a possibility. A new comparison uses the actual room's companions and asks which of the omitted and placed paintings belongs beside them. It is a relationship choice, not another preference vote.

The gates and ranking weights are editorial rules. They are not calibrated confidence estimates, proof of psychological causes, or an exhaustive statistical model of taste.

## Interaction and reading changes

Fresh continuous-appeal plans retain the final explicit dislike selection and replace the earlier duplicate with a targeted comparison. Already frozen plans keep their original questions.

Optional refinement remains limited to ten interactions. It can first compare rival explanations suggested by declared favourites, then investigate an actual museum choice, then use other eligible explanation or response-map checks. It stops when no useful question is available. Predictions, source responses and artwork reviews are frozen before the new answer. Familiar works can form a new comparison, but do not become independent discovery evidence.

The invitation appears above the reading and remains available after the first completed block even if a later block has begun. Typed constructs and the unease axis direction are preserved when generating maps. An eligible selector does not establish that a participant actually saw the invitation.

The whole-profile opening combines up to two defensible connections and displays actual favourite works. Individual findings retain their own source scope. Unresolved categories remain visible as compact next comparisons rather than empty findings. Explicitly disliked evidence is labelled as a counterexample or contrast, with an explanation of why it appears.

Collection actions can add declared quiz favourites, reset saved paintings, or undo the reset without changing quiz answers. The opening field has a bounded damped response after pointer movement ends.

## Research shortlist for better comparisons

These are proposed curatorial uses, not additions in this change. Recognition is a rationale for selecting candidates, not an assumed response from any participant.

| Work | Availability | Proposed comparison and uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grant Wood, American Gothic, 1930 | Existing `study-wood-american-gothic` | The Two Fridas: frontal paired portraits versus visible symbolic connection. Multiple figures alone should not establish interpersonal exchange. |
| Goya, The Third of May 1808 in Madrid, 1814 | Existing `canon-goya-third-may` | Saturn: whether appeal extends from grotesque fantasy to a recognisable human tragedy by the same artist. Lighting, arrangement and narrative still differ. |
| Hopper, Nighthawks, 1942 | New asset needed | The Night Café: illuminated night interiors, atmosphere and differing interpersonal clues. Palette, geometry and familiarity remain alternatives. |
| Rivera, Dream of a Sunday Afternoon in Alameda Central, 1947 | Existing `req-rivera-alameda` | Bosch's Garden, corroborated by Controller of the Universe: dense narrative organisation versus fantastical invention. Requires a full overview and useful zoom. |
| Picasso, Guernica, 1937 | New asset needed | Collective Suicide: fragmented tragic scenes across different palettes and representation. Ask admiration, felt unease and return separately; do not infer them from the subject. |

Primary sources: [Art Institute, American Gothic](https://www.artic.edu/assets/f85bee6b-d18b-5577-a9e6-f429421b4654); [Fundación Goya, Third of May](https://fundaciongoyaenaragon.es/eng/obra/el-tres-de-mayo-de-1808/181); [Art Institute, Nighthawks](https://archive.artic.edu/hopper/artwork/111628); [INBAL, Alameda](https://museomuraldiegorivera.inba.gob.mx/images/PDF/-HOJA-DE-SALA-MMDR.pdf); [Reina Sofía, Guernica](https://www.museoreinasofia.es/en/collections/artwork/guernica-0/).

For subsequent asset work, record exact reproduction identity and rights separately from artwork identity. The Art Institute documents United States public-domain status for Nighthawks but not universal CC0 status. No open reproduction permission was established here for Guernica.

No single pair isolates a visual quality. These proposals earn their place when they challenge a current explanation, preserve absolute placements and gaps, and lead to proportionate wording with named exceptions.
