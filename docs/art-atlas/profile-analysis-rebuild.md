# Profile analysis, November 2026

The profile describes several supported connections and their exceptions. It does not select one of seven annotations as a visitor's identity.

## Preference evidence

`profile-evidence.mjs` validates original question/answer identities. A conflicting `workIds` alias, duplicate round, partial dictionary or unconfirmed answer supplies no evidence. Complete frozen artwork objects retain their own annotations; a current sidecar cannot upgrade an archived answer.

Only `overall-appeal-v1`, its exact anchors and the `overall-appeal` scope permit comparison of recorded overall appeal across sets. The latest value per painting remains available with its full response history. Repeated values are not averaged or counted as new artworks. The 65/35 positive/lower bands are provisional descriptive rules, not confidence, percentiles or validated population cutoffs.

Explicit `overall-favourites` selections come first in `heroWorks`; those selections are unranked. Next come comparable overall ratings. Old relative spectra supply labelled local leaders, with no pooled numerical favourite ranking or alphabetical tie breaker. If all recorded overall ratings are low, the highest examples have `positive:false`; they must not be labelled favourites. Saves, curation, recognition and generic keep grids are not preference ratings.

Paged favourites use validated page presentation. Unvisited paintings are neither non-selections nor evidence against an earlier favourite. A paged record missing its presentation metadata cannot prove a new pair was unseen.

## Portrait result fields

Existing result fields remain. New fields are:

- `patternCandidates`: every qualifying seven-feature candidate, with full discovery, later-comparison and repeat balances. Supporting and opposing pair directions within the same set remain visible. Candidates sharing supporting pairs may share one displayed observation. Neither priority nor pair count is confidence.
- `preferenceEvidence`: instrument basis, scope availability, source question IDs, individual overall ratings and histories, and limits.
- `responsePortrait` and `reportedGridMaps`: all valid achievement, reading and return maps under their original definitions. Repeated works remain separate points. Exact known semantics are required for interpretation; a differently labelled map can remain a literal display.
- `facets`: reviewed connections among positive choices, including counts of reviewed positive and lower-rated works. Each review must bind its source, reproduction and checksum. Missing reviews are unknown. Profile naming needs a qualified favourite connection or a descriptive concentration against known lower-rated works; a common offered subject does not create an identity.
- `themes` and `profileSummary`: all qualified descriptive themes remain available; the summary uses at most two, with explicit-favourite connections first. Legacy local comparisons and direct map relationships retain their distinct basis. Positive density breadth remains an observation and never names the profile.
- `closingRefinement`: frozen primary/rival checks, favourite refinements, observations and coverage. Visible checks sharing a primary feature are consolidated into one specific reading; `roundObservations` retains every original per-question finding. Question, unique-pair and raw-check counts remain separate. Two pair checks inside one four-work answer are dependent.

New findings retain `support`, `counter`, `alternatives`, actual work IDs and source round IDs. `displayWorkIds` names the actual paintings described in the short text; it does not rank them. Range notes distinguish a sparse/dense composition and an explicit favourite/positive overall response.

## Readings and discovery

`response-portrait.mjs` adds recurring appeal/admiration alignment with separations retained, positive choices linked to clarity and openness, and a wish to return to both comforting and unsettling works. These are recorded judgments or intentions, not causes or observed viewing behaviour. Distinct artwork-set and work thresholds prevent repeated pairs from manufacturing recurrence.

`facet-readings.mjs` uses eight nonexclusive reviewed artwork descriptions. A descriptive connection is not automatically a preference separator or a named identity. Lower-rated works sharing its tags, positive works outside it and review coverage remain available. An explicitly reviewed empty tag set is not a missing review or an assertion that a work lacks meaning.

`closing-readings.mjs` verifies the whole four-work answer, at least three distinct earlier frozen source sets and four named artists, actual source identities and measurement scope, captured directional values, test descriptors and primary/rival predictions. Claimed novel pairings require actual earlier presentation evidence, including opened pages and museum mounting. Unknown visibility suppresses novelty. Explicitly nonnovel checks stay descriptive. Later source-answer edits cannot refit the captured hypothesis. A selected favourite may refine a higher earlier rating only when the alternative was actually presented in that favourites question; non-selection is not dislike.

Artist, period and tradition routes retain supporting and opposing distinct-set counts, including works that never became positive seeds. Museum routes rank actual explicit-favourite holding concentration before other positive holdings and artist-only connections. Every sourced route remains available; there is no analysis-side first-three museum cutoff or claim about current exhibition. Painting suggestions rank their direct connections before incidental catalogue order.

The portrait retains all valid curatorial findings in priority order. Connections retain every validated group, pair link, omission record and cross-instrument comparison. Six-work thumbnail previews retain `allWorkIds` and full evidence. Gallery membership and normalized 3:2 sorting distance never become one shared similarity scale.

## Coverage and checks

The canonical catalogue contains 41 reading families: 29 current, 7 retained-legacy and 5 proposed. Of these, 36 have mapped runtime sources. The explorer uses the existing six lenses; illustrative examples are never visitor results. Older density, figures, openness and unexpectedness appraisals remain literal retained records; current attention-to-making is separate from admiration.

Behavioural tests cover positive and opposing evidence, all-high responses, sparse/dense breadth, explicit favourites, unvisited pages, lower-rated artist/period counters, museum concentration, exact map labels, frozen source values, rival checks, unknown visibility, malformed identities and real reviewed artworks. The privately retained original fifty-answer export is read only when present; its original bytes and legacy interpretation remain unchanged.
